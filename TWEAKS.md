## The 5 High-Impact Tweaks to Unlock Sub-90

### 1. Add a 3rd Weekly Run (40–50 Min Easy Zone 2) — *Primary Bottleneck*

* **What the data shows:** Since 2023, you have run almost exclusively **2 days per week** (Monday/Tuesday evening speed/tempo workout + Thursday evening long run), averaging **1.82 runs/week** and **22.4 km (13.9 mi)/week** in 2026, while getting extra aerobic volume from cycling (95.6 hrs at 126 bpm avg HR in 2026) and e-biking (56.6 hrs).
* **Why it limits your Half Marathon:** Your cycling base gives you strong central cardiovascular durability up to ~70 minutes: on steady runs up to **9.5 miles (15.3 km)**, your aerobic decoupling (cardiac drift between the first half and second half) is only **2.9%–4.1%** (`2026-02-19` and `2026-03-05`). However, on every steady long run of **10 to 11+ miles (16–18 km)**, your decoupling rises above the 5% fatigue threshold to **+5.3% to +7.6%** (`+7.6%` on `2025-11-20`, `+6.7%` on `2026-05-07`, `+5.3%` on `2026-07-23`, `+6.0%` on `2026-10-22`). Cycling does not replicate the eccentric muscular/tendon loading of 18,000+ footstrikes over 21.1 km.
* **Actionable Tweak:** Add **one 40–50 minute easy Zone 2 run (8–10 km / 5–6 mi at 140–150 bpm)** on Saturday morning or Wednesday evening. Pushing your weekly running frequency from 2 days to **3 days/week (~32–40 km / 20–25 mi per week)** will build the lower-leg durability needed to hold `4:15/km` (`6:51/mi`) from mile 10 to 13.1.

### 3. Extend Continuous Threshold Volume & Peak Long Runs (13–14 Miles)

* **What the data shows:**

* **Actionable Tweak:**
  1. **Over-distance long runs:** Build your Thursday (or weekend) long run to **21–23 km (13–14.5 miles)** at least 3–4 times during your peak 10-week build.
